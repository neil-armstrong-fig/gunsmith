# AGENTS.md: engine

`engine/` is the rules of gunsmith: a deterministic simulation, a package of its own so the webapp now, and the API's
Worker later, run the same code. Pure TypeScript: no React, no DOM, no clock, no
`Math.random`, no I/O. A world and an input go in; the next world comes out, and the same two always give the same answer.

Inside the package a file imports another by the package's own name, `@gunsmith/engine/arena/Arena`
(an `@src` alias would resolve into whichever package imports it). `webapp`, `ai` and the future `api` may import it; acceptance specs may not.
It is not in `shared/` because `acceptance-tests/` may import anything there, and a spec that recomputed its expectation
from the engine would agree with it whatever the engine did.

## What is here

One folder per subject. Each has its entry points at the top, its types in `types/`, and a subfolder for a helper only an
entry point uses. Several entry points may share a root only when they share the subject (`match/` has five, all the match);
a file that does not share it is a different domain with a folder of its own, and a helper with one caller goes beneath that
caller (`opponent/state/acting/engage/aim/` serves `Engage`). `src/` follows the same rule: its one file is `Engine.ts`, and
everything else is a domain folder. Tiles are the unit; `x` grows to the east and `y` to the north.

The package front door is `Engine.ts` (`startingWorld` and `step`), not a barrel. A match between two people is `game-loop/StepFighters.ts` (`step` and it share the core `PlayStep.ts`), and what each seat may be told is `seat/` (`ViewOfSeat` is the server's fog filter, built on `visibleToPlayer` over `MirrorSeats`; `WorldOfSeatView` is its inverse for the browser). A mutual knockout in one step is a drawn round (`RoundState` `"drawn"`, `round/DecideRound`): nobody scores (`ScoreRound`), `MirrorSeats` leaves it drawn, and with no opponent a fallen player still loses.
Use the current code and adjacent tests to find a domain's implementation.

`Step`'s tests are split by subject in `game-loop/step/` (`StepWalking`, `StepFiring`, `StepDummies`, `StepWeapons`, `StepRound`, `StepOpponent`, `StepRing`, `StepPickup`, `StepEvents`, each a `.test.ts`), because one file of a hundred cases was too long to read; their shared fixtures (`TICK`, `IDLE`, `arena`, `walking`, `centred`, `sparring` and the rest) are one export per file in `game-loop/step/testing/`. A new case goes in the file for its subject, not in a new catch-all.

Several entry points here have a single caller (`AdvanceBullets`, `AimPlayer`, `TriggerPull`, `PushOutOfWalls` are called
only by `step` or `MovePlayer`). They stay in domain folders of their own, not beneath that caller, on purpose: these are
the engine's vocabulary, each will grow (hits, health, dummies) and be used from more than one place, and `engine/` is
imported by other packages. A single-caller helper that is _not_ a domain still goes beneath its caller.

A new subject (`ring/`, `sound/`) gets a folder of its own rather than joining a bucket. Past about six
files a folder is a smell: split it by subject.

## Conventions

- **A new rule gets a unit test first** (`it(...)` at the top level, no `describe`), watched to fail, then mutation-proved.
  Wall, edge and corner cases are where this package has been wrong before; test each side, not one.
- **The engine does not trust its input.** `step` caps the direction's length, so a client cannot walk faster by asking.
- **Audit the folder before saying done.** For each new or changed domain folder, list its files and, for every root file, count its
  callers (`grep -rln "<domain>/<Name>\"" engine/src webapp/src`): one caller means it goes in a subject-named subfolder beneath that
  caller; two or more domains means it stays at the owning root. Every interface is in `<domain>/types/`. A root of more than about five
  entry points needs a reason (`vision/` has five, all about seeing). Move a file with its test, with plain `mv`.
- **A step never changes the world it is given**; it returns a new one.
- **Tunables are constants in their own file** (`PlayerSpeed.ts`) until there are enough to want a table.
- Property tests use `fast-check` and run separately through `pnpm test:properties`; `PROPERTY_TEST_RUNS` sets the cases. Preserve seed/counterexample output. `power-rating/` and `weapon-balance/` own deterministic stat costs and downward balancing, including deliberate weakness. A weapon has eight stats: its six numbers and its vision, the cone's width and reach (`visionCone` and `visionRange` in `STAT_NAMES`; `visionDegrees` 50 to 130 and `visionRange` 6 to 20 in `STAT_RANGES`), each paid for in the rating (shares 0.05 and 0.10, linear goodness; the rifle rates about 0.53). These initial coefficients require gameplay calibration.

Stair arenas use deterministic `route/` walking paths for wandering, searching and ring shelter. Flat arenas retain existing steering. Arena parsing rejects raised stair flanks that bypass landings. Engagement and cover work across levels: `EngageDirection(arena, …)` judges range by the player but closes in toward `RouteWaypoint`, so a drop is gone round by its stair; `NearestCover` hides a place by the sight rule (`hidesFrom`, the edge rule: a place back from a platform's edge is hidden from the low ground, and the low ground from a threat back from the edge) and ranks it by the walk there (`WalkingDistanceTo`); `Reload` walks to cover by `RouteWaypoint`. A bot that sees the player from a stair or an edge within its normal range fights from where it is (the user's choice) and routes only to close in or reach cover. The `seek` state (`state/acting/Seek.ts`) walks to a weapon lying in the arena, by stair if need be, without firing; `ChooseState` sends the bot there from any state, in sight of the player or not, when `state/seeking/WinningTheRace` says it is nearer by walk (`state/walking/WalkingDistanceTo`) by `seekLead` tiles, and keeps it there until the player is nearer by more than `seekGrace` (`Difficulty`, both tunables for play). An empty magazine still reloads first. A bullet of the player's that hurts the opponent tells its brain where the player stood (`opponent/RememberShot`, called by `PlayStep`; ring damage never does), so a shooter it cannot see, out of its sight range, behind it or on higher ground, is searched for: `noticeShot` makes that place the last place it knew the player to be and `ChooseState` starts (or restarts) `search` for it; `ThinkOpponent` clears the shot once the state is chosen.

`arena-symmetry/` makes starts fair without changing `Arena`: `isHalfTurnSymmetric` says whether an arena is the same picture turned half way round, `spawnPairsOf` lists the tile pairs a half turn takes onto one another (open floor, one level, no stair, reachable, `SPAWN_SPACING_STEPS` apart), `chooseStartingPlaces(arena, seed)` draws one pair and who takes which end from the seed on its own `SPAWN_SEED_SALT` stream (never an unmatched pair; `undefined` when there is none), and `startAtPlaces` stands both fighters there facing the middle. The ring centre and the pickup stay random and are not filtered. Half-turn only so far; reflection needs its own aim and input checks. `fairStart(arena, seed)` is the one call for a round's starting world on any arena: `chooseStartingPlaces` when the arena has a pair, else the pairs of `spawn-pairs/matched-distances/MatchedDistancePairs` (open floor on one level, `SPAWN_SPACING_STEPS` apart on foot, the most nearly the same walk from the middle, or the longest walk there is for a small arena), drawn by `DrawStartingPlaces` from the same `SPAWN_SEED_SALT` stream; `undefined` only with fewer than two places to stand. It sets up no ring, pickup or brain: the API's `MatchRoom` calls it with a fresh seed each round and adds `startRing` and `startPickup` from the same seed (the submachine gun lies fair between the seats).

**The edge rule** (2026-11-26): `vision/sight/HasClearSight` is the one sight test between two points (`canSeePoint`, so fighters, dummies, the bot, the seat's fog and `HidesFrom`): on one level as before; between levels raised floor may lie on the line only within `EDGE_REACH_TILES` (`height/EdgeReach`, 1) of the higher end, judged low end up so it is symmetric (`vision/sight/property-tests/`). `visibleArea` is an outline and hidden patches inside it: `vision/outline/StretchesSeenAlong` walks each ray once with running floor heights, pinning each edge with `hasClearSight`, so a same-level platform past unseen low ground is lit; `HiddenPatches` keeps patches inside the outline and apart (the veil never dims twice). Bullets agree: `DistanceToStop` lets one fly `EDGE_REACH_TILES - PLAYER_RADIUS` onto higher ground, and `Bullet.floor` (set by `bullet/shot-floor/FloorOfShot`, raised by `bullet/flight/raised-floor/RaisedFloor` on each step up) passes over anyone below it; no origin is carried.

**Low cover** (2026-11-18): `Arena.cover` marks tiles drawn `c`, always a subset of `solid`, on the low ground, so walking, routing, placement and `validateArena` treat a block as a wall unchanged and only sight reads `cover`. `blocksSightFrom(arena, eye)` takes an `Eye` (`eyeLevel`, `crouching`; `Cone` extends it): a block stops a crouching viewer no higher than it, never a standing one. `canSeeFighter` adds the target's stance (`coverBesideTarget`, `COVER_REACH_TILES`): a crouched fighter with a block on the line within that reach is hidden from every viewer, if on the block's level (`eyeLevelAt`). `canSeePoint` stays for places with no stance; `Dummy.crouching` is static, and `hurtDummies` must keep a dummy's other fields. A bullet knows only the shooter's stance (`Bullet.crouching`, set by `fireBullet`; no origin, so a seat's view carries nothing about where it was fired from, pinned in `ViewOfSeat.test`): a crouched shooter's bullet is stopped by a cover block no higher than it (`bullet/flight/stopping/DistanceToStop` asks `blocksSightFrom` with that stance), a standing one's flies over. `isShieldedByCover(arena, direction, target)` reads the bullet's straight path back from a crouched target (`FlightRequest.playerCrouching`/`opponentCrouching`, `Dummy.crouching`) and says whether cover stands within `COVER_REACH_TILES` of them on it, the same test as sight; a shielded fighter or dummy is passed over, not hit, and the bullet flies on. Known limit: a shooter touching a block right behind them could shield a target touching their muzzle; not worth a state field. **The bot ducks** (2026-11-23): `Engage` crouches and holds still while it rests between bursts (reaction time over, trigger up) when `coverBesideTarget` is true from the player to it, and stands for its reaction time and every shot, since a crouched shooter's bullets stop at the block; `Reload` crouches and holds where it stands when seen with a block beside it and no other cover chosen; `ThinkOpponent` judges the bot's own sight as if standing (a ducked bot peeks, or its own block would blind it and it would leave `engage`). `shelterFromRing` stands the bot up to walk (a crouched walk, 0.4 of the pace, could not outrun the edge). **It walks to one** (2026-11-25): `findCover` adds the middle of every open tile beside a block within `coverReach` (`cover/TilesBesideCover`, shared with `HidingPlacesOf`) to its random tries, `NearestCover` takes a place `reload/cover/HidesFrom` says hides (walls, height or a block), and `Reload` crouches on arriving and looks again once flanked (its cover no longer hides it). **`cover/HidingPlacesOf`** (2026-11-23) is the player's hint of where a crouched fighter could hide: `hidingPlacesOf(arena, cone)` gives the quarter-tile squares (`HidingPlace {x, y, size}`) next to each cover block that `coverBesideTarget` hides from the cone's apex and `canSeePoint` shows, so the webapp's `CoverShade` draws the engine's rule and never its own. It asks the cheap one-tile cover question before the long sight ray (3 ms for 150 blocks and a wide cone, measured) and says nothing of where anyone is.

`built-in-arenas/` is the one list of arenas the game ships (`BUILT_IN_ARENAS`, each a `{name, rows}` drawing that `arenaFrom` reads; the webapp reads it; ai and the API are to drop their own copies, see HANDOFF.md) and `arena-limits/` holds the size bounds an arena in it, or one a model draws, must fit (`ARENA_LIMITS`, width and height, tunables for play). A new pool entry is one file and one line in the list (Duel and Fair duel sit at the folder's root because api and webapp import them; every other drawing is read only by the list and goes in `built-in-arenas/drawings/`), and `built-in-arenas/BuiltInArenas.test.ts` holds every entry to reading, `validateArena`, the limits and half-turn symmetry. A scenario's own fixture arena is neither in the pool nor held to the size limits. `validateArena` also refuses an arena with nowhere to start both fighters (`noStartingPlaces`: no stretch of floor has two places to stand on one level, which is all `fairStart` needs; it asks every region, not the largest, because `fairStart`'s middle tile may sit in a cut-off pocket and the answer would then depend on which way the arena is turned; `arena-symmetry/IsPlaceToStand` is shared with the spawn pairs) and one with too little open floor (`tooWalled`, last in its list: floor under `ARENA_LIMITS.floorShare.min`, 25% of the tiles, a cover block counting as wall; `arena-validation/floor-share/FloorShareOf`). A maze of one-tile passages is legal, so there is no corridor rule.

`arena-rotation/` is the daily set of built-in arenas for private rooms (the user's design, 2026-11-24): `arenasOfDay({day, arenas, plays})` returns `size` (5) names, the drawn ones first and the kept ones last (each in the pool's order, so the room's second row is yesterday's): up to `mostPlayed` (2) of the arenas most played **yesterday** (a play is a match begun; an arena nobody played yesterday is never kept, ties go to the pool's order) and the rest drawn on the day's own salted stream, each weighted `1 / (1 + plays in the last recentDays (7) days)`, so a recently played arena is less likely; after a day nobody played, all five are drawn afresh (the user, 2026-11-25). Only plays before `day` count, so the set holds all day and any server computes the same one; `ARENA_ROTATION` holds the tunables. The caller counts plays and works out the day (days since 1970 UTC); nothing here reads a clock or storage.

`built-in-weapons/BuiltInWeapons.ts` is the one list of weapons the game ships (`BUILT_IN_WEAPONS`: rifle, marksman rifle, submachine gun, picked by `identity.key`); the API's room and the webapp's pre-round read it, and its test holds every entry to a key and name of its own and to `STAT_RANGES`. A new entry is its own file in `weapon/weapons/` and one line in the list.

`arena-picking/` holds `resolveArenaPicks({picks, seed})`, the pure rule that says whose arena is played from what the two players chose (agreed, a coin, the only pick, or a draw for pick or random), on its own `ARENA_PICK_SEED_SALT` stream; it was ai's and moved here because the webapp may not import `ai/`. `match/ForfeitRound.ts` scores a round for the seat that stayed; the timing that decides a forfeit is the API's.

`library-items/` reads what the library kept, which is `unknown` until here: `parseStoredWeapon(value)` turns a stored `{name, six stats, optionally visionDegrees and visionRange}` into an equippable `Weapon` (zod, extra keys dropped, each number inside `STAT_RANGES`, a whole magazine and reserve, then `withinLimits` with vision counted; identity key is always `"saved"` because the other seat is told a key, and a vision number left out is the rifle's, which is every weapon kept before vision was a stat), and `parseStoredArena(value)` turns `{name, rows}` into `{name, rows, arena}` (rows inside `ARENA_LIMITS`, `arenaFrom`, then `validateArena` empty). Both return `undefined` on any fault and never throw. Appearance is not engine's: the API parses it with shared's `appearanceSchema`. Property test: `library-items/property-tests/`.

**Events and sound:** `World.events` is what happened to the player in the last step and `World.opponentEvents` is the same for the opponent, built by the same `eventsOfStep` in `PlayStep` (the opponent's hits are judged by the sight rule through `mirrorSeats`, so the two seats cannot differ); `mirrorSeats` swaps them and `viewOfSeat` tells a seat only its own as `SeatView.events`, never about the other fighter. The one thing it says of an unseen fighter is `HeardEvent` (`sound/HeardInStep`: their shot, footfall (`sound/footsteps/FootstepOf`: the first moving tick upright and every 0.42 s of continuous walking) or weapon taken, each within its own `HEARING_RANGES`, as `{sound, sector, loudness}`: one of eight sectors from `sound/direction/SectorOf`, 0 north then clockwise, and near, mid or far from `sound/loudness/LoudnessOf`, nothing else; the browser turns those into the ears), pinned by `ViewOfSeat.test` and the api's fog property. A caller that steps more than once per snapshot (the room's tick) must gather each step's events itself.

`arena-repair/` (2026-11-20) holds `repairArena(rows)`: the plain-rule repair of the faults a model most often draws into an arena, with no model call, which `ai/`'s `validateArenaDraft` runs before `validateArena` judges (it returns `{rows, edits}`, and no edits means the drawing was left as it was). In order: a stair `arenaFrom` refuses is drawn as floor (`stairs/DropIllegalStairs`, each judged alone, then all together); a cut-off raised region gets a stair beside it on floor that is part of the arena (`stairs/AddStair`), or, for a level-2 region standing on the ground, a landing between two stairs in a straight line on arena floor (`stairs/AddLanding`, edit `landingAdded`, 2026-11-24: Haiku draws level 2 with no landing, and lowering it lost the height a player asked for), or, for a level-2 top ringed by a first level too thin for a stair (its ground lies behind any stair), one ground tile in line is raised to the first level and the ring tile between becomes a stair (`stairs/WidenPlatform`, edit `platformWidened`, 2026-11-27, written by ai: Haiku's watchtower towers; the only edit that raises ground), or is lowered a level (the climb table is `stairs/climbs/Climbs`); a cut-off piece of low floor of at most 12 tiles and a tenth of the floor is walled in, and a bigger one gets the cheapest way cut through walls to the arena (`doors/OpenDoor`, over `doors/cheapest-way/CheapestWay`, which never crosses the border, a stair or a platform). It mends one piece at a time (at most 100; a realistic repair took 2 to 4 ms in Node, a drawing of nothing but faults about a second at a cap of 200, the bound at 100 unmeasured) and checks each result with `arenaFrom`; a drawing it cannot read is returned as it came, and playability stays `validateArena`'s to judge. `POCKET_MOST_TILES` and `POCKET_MOST_SHARE_OF_FLOOR` (`RepairArena.ts`) are tunables for play. Its properties (`property-tests/`, in `PROPERTY_TESTS`): it keeps the size and border, leaves no piece cut off whenever it can read the result, never makes a readable drawing unreadable, leaves a one-piece drawing alone, and is finished after one pass.
